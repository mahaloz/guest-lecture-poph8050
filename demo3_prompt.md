# Research Task: Investigating Avian Influenza Outbreak Patterns in the United States

You are an autonomous AI research assistant investigating Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza (HPAI) outbreaks in the United States.

**Your primary research question:**

What patterns can we discover in the historical occurrence of avian influenza outbreaks in U.S. poultry, and what might explain those patterns?

I want you to conduct an end-to-end scientific investigation, not simply summarize existing publications.

## Phase 1: Discover and Acquire Data

1. Find official historical avian influenza outbreak data from the USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS).
2. Prioritize confirmed detections in commercial and backyard poultry flocks, ideally covering 2022 through the latest available date.
3. Download the datasets and document their sources, coverage, and limitations.
4. Investigate what other public datasets might help explain outbreak patterns, such as wild-bird surveillance data or seasonal information.

## Phase 2: Form Research Hypotheses

Before analyzing the data, propose at least three testable hypotheses.

For example:
- Are outbreaks more frequent during certain seasons?
- Do commercial and backyard poultry flocks exhibit different temporal patterns?
- Do outbreak patterns differ geographically?
- Are trends in wild-bird detections associated with subsequent poultry detections?

You may formulate other interesting hypotheses based on your literature review and available data.

## Phase 3: Conduct the Investigation

Write and execute Python code to:

1. Clean, normalize, and validate the acquired datasets.
2. Perform exploratory data analysis.
3. Generate informative graphs and geographic visualizations.
4. Test your hypotheses using appropriate statistical methods.
5. Investigate unexpected patterns and revise your hypotheses when justified.

Clearly distinguish detection events, affected flocks, and affected birds. Do not assume reported detections represent all infections or calculate infection rates without suitable denominators.

## Phase 4: Critically Evaluate Your Findings

For each hypothesis:

- Explain your methodology.
- Present quantitative evidence.
- Determine whether the evidence supports the hypothesis.
- Identify possible confounding variables and surveillance biases.
- Explain why correlations do not necessarily imply causation.

Compare your findings with relevant peer-reviewed research and identify disagreements or unresolved questions.

## Phase 5: Produce Research Artifacts

Create the following deliverables:

1. **Research report:** A scientific report with an abstract, background, hypotheses, methodology, results, discussion, limitations, and references.
2. **Figures:** At least five publication-quality visualizations supporting the analysis.
3. **Reproducible code:** Python scripts and dependency instructions sufficient to reproduce the analysis.
4. **Research notebook:** A Jupyter notebook documenting the investigation step by step.
5. **Future research:** Three promising follow-up questions motivated by the findings.

Save all artifacts in a well-organized project directory.

## Research Integrity Requirements

- Use real, publicly accessible data. Never fabricate observations, references, or statistical results.
- Cite sources and record dataset retrieval dates.
- Clearly distinguish established findings from your own hypotheses or speculation.
- Include the actual executed analysis and its results, not merely proposed code.
- If data is missing, inaccessible, or unsuitable, explain the limitation and adapt your methodology.
- Do not overstate the statistical significance or causal meaning of observed patterns.
- Restrict the investigation to observational epidemiology and public data analysis.

**Work autonomously.** Make reasonable methodological decisions, explain them in your report, and continue through the entire investigation unless an essential blocker prevents further progress.

Your goal is to produce a credible, reproducible scientific investigation that a graduate student in avian virology could critically review.

## Completion

When completed, you must make the results visually inspectable in some way on a webpage. I want to visually understand what you did so I can explain it to others in whatever the findings where.
